# How real recipes write amounts

Collected 2026-11-04 to make the game's recipe cards read like the real thing. Several big recipe sites (Allrecipes, Betty Crocker, Simply Recipes, Sally's Baking Addiction, Budget Bytes) block automated reading, so these notes come from the pages that could be read, plus abbreviation lists.

## Three styles you'll see

| Style | Where | Example lines |
|---|---|---|
| **Foods-lab handout** | school and university lab sheets ([BYU Foods](https://byufoods.blogspot.com/p/management-lab.html), [Oklahoma Cooking for Kids](https://cookingforkids.ok.gov/?p=229093)) | `3 C. flour` · `1 ½ c. all-purpose flour` · `3 Tbsp. butter, softened` · `1 tsp. pure vanilla extract` · `4 oz. low fat cream cheese` · `½ lb. white chocolate` · `1 pt.` · `1 qt.` |
| **Recipe website** | [King Arthur Baking](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/blueberry-muffins-recipe) | `2 1/4 cups (270g) all-purpose flour` · `1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder` · `4 tablespoons (57g) unsalted butter` · `2 large eggs` |
| **Classic / family cookbook** | community cookbooks and abbreviation keys ([Family Cookbook Project](https://www.familycookbookproject.com/theblog/?p=2532), [OSU 4-H list](https://migrate-4h.okstate.edu/projects/nutrition-health-wellness/site-files/docs/food-showdown-pdfs/common-abbreviations-used-in-cooking.pdf)) | `t.` teaspoon · `T.` tablespoon · `C.` cup · `oz.` · `lb.` · `pkg.` |

## Patterns
- **The same recipe often mixes spellings:** `1 ½ c. all-purpose flour` and `1 ½ C. sugar`; `1 Tbsp. water` and `1 Tbsp vanilla`. Students must recognize all of them.
- **Periods are common on lab sheets:** `tsp.`, `Tbsp.`, `C.`, `oz.`, `lb.`, `pt.`, `qt.`
- **Fractions dominate**, written both as `½` and `1/2`, with a space in mixed numbers (`1 ½`, `2 1/4`).
- **Qualifiers follow a comma:** `butter, softened` · `butter, melted` · `2 eggs, beaten` · `firmly packed light brown sugar` · `2 large eggs` · `fresh or frozen`.
- **Websites put grams in parentheses** after cups (`2 1/4 cups (270g)`). Lab sheets don't.
- **Typical amounts:** salt ¼–1½ tsp.; baking powder ½ tsp.–4 tsp.; baking soda ½–1½ tsp.; vanilla 1 tsp.–1 Tbsp.; butter 3 Tbsp.–1 C.; flour 1½–3 C.; sugar ¾–1½ C.; milk ½–1 C.

## Scaling in real school recipes
Oklahoma's school muffin base comes in 6, 24, 48, and 96 servings: **×4, then ×2, then ×2**. Scaled amounts get consolidated up into bigger units:
- 3 tbsp. → ¾ c. → 1 ½ c.
- ½ c. milk → 1 pt. → 1 qt. → 2 qt.
- salt 1 tsp. → 2 tsp. → 1 tbsp., 1 tsp.
- flour 3 ¾ oz. → 15 oz. → 1 lbs., 14 oz.

Real scaling is mostly ×½ and ×2, sometimes ×3 or ×4, and good cooks consolidate to fewer, bigger measures.

## What changed in the game
- Recipe cards now come in all three styles (lab `C.`/`Tbsp.`/`tsp.`, website `cups`/`tablespoons`/`teaspoons`, classic `c`/`T`/`t`) with qualifiers such as "softened", "packed", and "large".
- Recipe-card factors are mostly ×½ and ×2, sometimes ×3 or ×4.
